# bom-source-backed-research

## When to Use

- The answer depends on niche, current, technical, legal, medical, product, or market information.
- The user requests research, verification, links, or citations.

## Required Inputs

- Research question.
- Required date range and geography.
- Decision the research must support.
- Preferred depth and source constraints.

## Procedure

1. Break the question into factual subclaims and decision criteria.
2. Search current primary sources first: official documentation, repositories, standards, papers, regulators, or company disclosures.
3. Use reputable secondary sources only to add context or competing interpretations.
4. Record source date, publication date, event date, and version where relevant.
5. Cross-check load-bearing claims with more than one source when practical.
6. Separate sourced facts, calculations, assumptions, and inference.
7. Synthesize the answer around the user’s decision, not around the order sources were found.

## Decision Rules

- Do not cite a source that does not support the attached claim.
- Do not use old data as current without labeling it.
- Avoid long quotations; paraphrase accurately.
- State when evidence is incomplete or disputed.

## Pitfalls

- Collecting many sources without answering the decision.
- Citing search snippets instead of opening sources.
- Mixing event date with article publication date.
- Presenting inference as fact.

## Verification

- Key claims have relevant citations.
- Sources are current enough for the question.
- Conflicting evidence is represented.
- The conclusion follows from the cited evidence.

## Output Format

- Direct answer.
- Evidence and comparison.
- Inference and uncertainty.
- Practical recommendation.
